Papertalk Help

Suggested papers

Papertalk can find papers like the ones in your library, rank them by how well they fit, and import the ones you pick.

Turn it on

Click Suggested Papers in the sidebar (or choose File → Show Suggested Papers) then open Settings → Suggested Papers and switch on Check every day. It's off until you turn it on, because a check sends the DOIs of the papers in your library to Semantic Scholar. Nothing else about your papers is sent.

Once a day, while Papertalk is open, it asks Semantic Scholar for papers like up to 100 of your most recent ones. When new papers turn up, a notification says how many (a message at the bottom of the window if Papertalk is in front), and the sidebar row counts them. Click the notification to open the list. Check Now, in the bar at the top of Suggested Papers, checks right away, even with the daily check off. The section's subtitle says when it last checked.

Suggestions start from DOIs, so papers without one aren't used. Add a missing DOI with Edit paper. Papers already in your library, and papers you dismissed or imported, are never suggested again.

Add your Google Scholar alerts

Google Scholar has no way for apps to read its recommendations, but it emails them: Papertalk can read those emails. Click the Google Scholar alerts tab, then Set Up Scholar Alerts… (shown until alerts are set up), or open Settings → Suggested Papers:

  1. Create alerts at scholar.google.com: open the menu, then Alerts. A search, an author, new citations to your papers and Scholar's recommended articles all work.
  2. For Gmail, turn on 2-Step Verification, then make an app password at myaccount.google.com/apppasswords. Other mail providers work too: change the IMAP server and Port.
  3. Switch on Read my Google Scholar alert emails, type your Email address and paste the App password. It's kept in the Keychain.
  4. Choose how far to Look back, from 1 week to 1 year (2 weeks unless you change it). Alert emails older than that are never read.
  5. Click Test Connection. It signs in and says how many alert emails it found in that period.

Each check, Papertalk opens the mailbox read-only: it never marks, moves or deletes mail. It reads up to 20 alert emails it hasn't read before, newest first, so a long look-back is worked through over a few days. Papers from alerts are listed under the Google Scholar alerts tab, and a paper both sources suggest shows once. If a Gmail filter skips the inbox for alerts, set the Mailbox to [Gmail]/All Mail. With alerts set up, suggestions arrive even when no paper in your library has a DOI. Reading alerts isn't available in the Linux preview yet.

Read and pick

The bar at the top has a tab per source, each with how many papers it holds: Semantic Scholar (recommended from your library's papers) and Google Scholar alerts (from your alert emails). Each suggested paper is a row with its title, authors and year. If an LLM is set in Settings → LLM Settings, it ranks the list by how well each paper fits your library and adds a line on why, marked with sparkles. Without one, the list keeps Semantic Scholar's order.

Click a row to see the paper in the details panel on the right: every author, why it fits, its abstract and DOI. From there, Import it, Open in Browser to read more, or Dismiss it.

Each row says how its PDF arrives:

  • Free PDF: Papertalk downloads it itself.
  • Download in browser: you download it, as below.

Each row also has its own Import button, and a Dismiss button that hides the paper for good; right-click a row for the same actions. Pick how they're written with Import as in Settings → Suggested Papers: In-depth, Summary or Direct read. It starts as your last import's depth (and a summary uses that import's length and language). Import more papers while one is on its way and they wait their turn, marked Queued.

Get a paper through your browser

Papers are fetched one at a time, in a card above the list. When a paper has no free PDF, or its free link doesn't give a PDF, the card shows these steps:

  1. Click Open in Browser to open the paper's page.
  2. Download the PDF. Sign in through your library or institution if the page asks.
  3. Papertalk picks up the new PDF from your Downloads folder and imports it.

While it waits, the card shows Waiting for a new PDF in Downloads…. Only a PDF that arrives after the wait begins counts, and only once the browser has finished saving it. Skip leaves the paper in the list and moves to the next; Stop ends the imports, and the papers not imported stay in the list.

If the PDF doesn't look like the suggested paper (its title isn't on the first pages), it's imported anyway and a message says so. Delete it if it's the wrong paper.
